# ADR 0011: Reading the transactions in House reports

**Status:** accepted · **Date:** 2026-11-25

## Context

ADR 0010 brought House members' periodic transaction reports (PTRs) into the ledger as an index: who filed which report when, with a link to its PDF, but not what was traded. Of the 8,465 reports from 2013 to 2026, 6,003 are typed (generated by the House's filing system) and 2,462 are scanned paper. 5,936 of the typed ones are encrypted (Standard security handler, V2 R3, RC4 128-bit) with an empty user password and permissions that allow printing but not copying text; the newest are not encrypted at all. The reports are public records published for anyone to read; the law limits how they may be used (5 U.S.C. 13107: not commercially, not for credit ratings or soliciting money), not how they are read.

Reading past the no-copying permission was left to the maintainer. On 2026-11-25 the maintainer decided to read them, for the project's non-commercial use.

## Decision

- **A new crate, `oe-ptr`, depends on `lopdf`** (0.47, MIT, Rust 1.88, default features off: no threads, no clock). It opens and decrypts the PDFs and decodes their streams. It is the only crate with a PDF dependency, as `oe-store` is the only one with a database driver; the core crates stay std-only (ADR 0001). Writing a PDF engine (object parser, RC4 and MD5, CID fonts) was weighed and rejected: more code to get wrong than the table reading it would serve.
- **Text with positions** (`oe_ptr::text`): a small interpreter of the operators that place text (`cm`, `Tm`, `Td`/`TD`/`T*`, `Tf`, `Tj`/`TJ`/`'`/`"`, form XObjects via `Do`), with each font's ToUnicode map and glyph widths. The reports draw every character separately inside a form XObject, so position is the only structure there is.
- **The table** (`oe_ptr::table`): characters become lines and cells by position; a transaction starts on a line with a type (P, S, S (partial), E; a type glued to the asset or written in lower case by a small-caps font is split off) whose next cell starts with a date; below it come more of the asset's name, the rest of the amount (also across a page break), and labelled lines (`FILING STATUS:` / `F S:`, `DESCRIPTION:` / `D:`, `COMMENTS:`, `SUBHOLDING OF:` / `S O:`). Headings, the certification and footers are ignored. Each field's parsing has a unit test on invented lines in both the earlier and the later layout.
- **Stored** in migration `0011_house_transactions` (`house_transaction`, `house_report_read`): owner code (member, SP spouse, JT joint, DC dependent child; never a family member's name), asset as written, ticker and asset type code, transaction type, transaction and notification dates, the amount range as written with its lower and upper end, filing status, description, comments, and the account it sits in. **Not stored: the location field**, which can point to where someone lives. The table is derived: `oe ptr-load` replaces it, recording the reader's version, so a better reader replaces old readings.
- **Linked to companies by ticker** (`issuer_cik`) when exactly one company's latest Form 3/4/5 ticker is the one written; members' trades then appear on company pages and charts, and in the outcome study (`official.buy` / `official.sale`, public from the report's filing date).
- **Scanned reports are not read** (no OCR); they stay listed with their links.
- Nothing from the reports is committed or published; the app shows the use limits wherever it shows the trades.

## Consequences

- Measured on all 6,003 typed reports (2026-11-25): every report opened and gave transactions, 57,806 transactions in 15 seconds (release build), 2 without a readable amount; 38,037 linked to a company. Types: 29,032 purchases, 21,031 sales, 7,168 partial sales, 575 exchanges. Owners: 28,074 the member, 15,370 joint, 12,255 spouse, 2,107 child.
- Known limits: 67 transactions are dated after their report and 729 more than two years before it (typed that way, or misread; shown as filed); an amended report repeats the original's transactions, so a trade can appear twice; asset names from older small-caps fonts come out in mixed case ("apple Inc."), tickers are upper-cased.
- Senators' reports (eFD, HTML) and executive-branch reports are still out of scope (ADR 0010).
